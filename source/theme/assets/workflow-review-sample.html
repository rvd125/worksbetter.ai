What a useful review can give you.

Illustrative sample. This fictional job-to-invoice example shows the shape of a review output. It is not a completed client review, quote, delivery commitment or guarantee. Your agreed scope determines the final outputs.

1. The current work, mapped

Priority: Prepare an invoice from a completed job without repeatedly copying job details.

  1. A coordinator checks the completed job and its notes.
  2. Recorded labour, materials and pricing are checked.
  3. A draft invoice is prepared in the accounting tool.
  4. A person confirms scope and approves the draft.
  5. Unclear variations or missing information return to the job owner.

To confirm: Who owns each handover, where delays occur, which existing integration features already do the work, and what information is dependable.

3. Priorities with reasons

Check existing features first. Confirm your job-management and accounting integration settings before adding another tool.

Reduce predictable copying next. Reuse dependable fields and agreed rates. Keep the source record available for checking.

Use AI selectively. Consider interpretation of job notes only if the need is established. Do not allow uncertain interpretation to decide scope, price or approval.

4. A proposed workflow and safeguards

Completed job → verify required information → prepare a draft → human scope and price review → approved next action.

SituationRequired response
Duplicate or repeated triggerStop a second invoice; keep a visible reference.
Missing job or pricing informationPause and route to the responsible person.
Unapproved variationSeparate it from the approved scope for a person's decision.
Connection or model failureRetain the work and use the agreed manual recovery path.
Normal complete recordPrepare the draft; make the relevant checks visible.

5. A decision about implementation

  • Proceed, improve the existing process first, or defer.
  • Agree the smallest useful scope and its exclusions.
  • Confirm account ownership, permissions and integration access.
  • Agree price, dates and third-party costs before implementation.
  • Set test cases and measurable acceptance criteria.
  • Document ownership, recovery, handover and optional monitoring.

Example acceptance question: Can your team trace every prepared invoice back to the approved job information and recover safely from an exception?
